# BLOODSTREAM — ECONOMY, INVENTORY & PROGRESSION

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Economy goal
Money should matter for most of the game without becoming a grind. Prices reflect region, scarcity, company control and player reputation.

## Income
Primary income sources:
- bounties and contracts;
- hunting and animal materials;
- escort or recovery work;
- sale of legitimate goods;
- selected story rewards;
- information/evidence rewards in authored cases.

Optional criminal income must interact with the law system rather than acting as consequence-free loot farming.

## Spending
Meaningful sinks include weapons, ammunition, repairs/maintenance, clothing, food, lodging, horse/tack, medical care, information/bribes, transport and selected camp equipment.

## Inventory
Separate immediate character inventory from horse/camp storage. Avoid grid-tetris unless testing proves it adds value. Weight/bulk should create believable limits without inventory micromanagement.

## Progression
No traditional level number is required. Progress comes from:
- player mastery;
- better or better-suited equipment;
- learned routes and information;
- trusted contacts;
- improved horse familiarity;
- broader bounty access;
- selected character capabilities earned through story/use.

## Skills
If a skill system is used, it must unlock meaningful techniques rather than percentage inflation. Examples: faster practical reload handling, improved field treatment, new restraint options, better clue interpretation or camp efficiency.

## Loot
No colored rarity beams, random stat rolls or showers of disposable gear. Items exist because people carried or stored them.

## Inflation control
Late-game rewards must not make food, ammunition and lodging meaningless. Expensive specialist gear, transport, information and story expenses can absorb wealth without artificial scaling.
