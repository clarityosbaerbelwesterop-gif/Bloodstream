# BLOODSTREAM — HUNTING & TRACKING

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Purpose
Hunting teaches the same observational language used in bounty pursuit: terrain, sign, wind, patience and careful execution.

## Tracking vocabulary
Readable clues include footprints and hoofprints, disturbed soil, snapped vegetation, hair or fur, blood, feeding signs, bedding sites, water disturbance, calls and scavenger behavior.

Clues degrade through time and weather. Mud preserves some tracks; rain can destroy them; snow records movement but can later cover it.

## Player skill
Tracking quality should improve mostly through player knowledge and subtle character competence—not an RPG skill tree that turns clues into neon paths. Optional assists can increase contrast or directional support.

## Hunt loop
Observe region → identify sign → determine freshness/direction → approach with wind/cover in mind → choose whether to take the shot → recover the trail if needed → field process → use or sell materials.

## Shot quality
Accurate vital hits preserve material quality and reduce unnecessary suffering. Poor hits can create a follow-up trail and damaged yield. Weapon and ammunition appropriateness matter.

## Field processing
Skinning and butchering are physical and grounded but paced for repetition. First-time or rare-animal animations can be detailed; repeated actions may use shorter variants.

## Human tracking
The same system extends to people through boot prints, horse shoes, camp ash, tobacco, discarded food, wagon wheel patterns, blood, broken locks, witnesses and route logic.

## Tracking notebook
The journal can record learned patterns, target-specific signs and sketches without solving trails automatically.

## Anti-magic rule
No supernatural hunter vision. Any focus mode only accentuates clues the protagonist could plausibly perceive.
