# BLOODSTREAM — GUNPLAY, WEAPONS & DUELS

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Gunplay thesis
Firearms are loud, lethal mechanical objects—not stat sticks. A gunfight should feel dangerous before the first shot and decisive once it starts.

## Core handling
Model aim stability, recoil, recovery, weapon length, reload state, movement penalty and injury effects without turning play into a simulator. First-person inspection may exist for maintenance but core combat remains third-person.

## Weapon classes
Final roster is historically validated. Design categories:
- single-action revolvers;
- older/conversion sidearms where era allows;
- lever-action rifles/carbines;
- single-shot rifles;
- shotguns;
- selected repeaters/long guns appropriate to exact year;
- bow and culturally/historically appropriate hunting tools;
- knives and utility blades.

No modern rarity tiers. Individual weapons differ by model, condition, barrel/stock configuration, sights and ammunition.

## Condition
Dirt/water/neglect may reduce handling or reliability within believable limits. Maintenance should be occasional and satisfying, not constant busywork.

## Ammunition
Ammunition type matters where it creates real decisions. Do not create dozens of meaningless variants. Carry limits should make extended fights and remote travel relevant.

## Enemy behavior
Enemies seek cover, flee, surrender, flank when plausible, drag wounded allies in authored/high-tier behavior and react to suppression. Ordinary humans do not absorb repeated rifle hits.

## Damage
Location matters, but avoid arcade floating numbers. Hits can kill, incapacitate, cause bleeding or impair movement/weapon use. Medical and gore presentation are governed elsewhere.

## Duel system — signature feature
Duels are about reading intent, legality and timing—not a reflex QTE.

Phases:
1. **Approach:** establish distance, witnesses, sun/light, obstacles and social stakes.
2. **Read:** watch eyes, shoulders, hands, weight shifts, breathing and dialogue.
3. **Commit:** decide when to move toward the weapon. Drawing too early may make the player the aggressor; drawing too late risks death.
4. **Draw:** fast transition from holster to firing solution, affected by weapon, stance, injury and readiness.
5. **Shot:** precision under extreme time pressure.
6. **Aftermath:** witnesses, law, wound, silence and reaction matter.

## Multi-person standoff
Three-way confrontations use attention and threat assessment. The player cannot simply slow time and mark every target. A rival may shoot another participant, negotiate or switch threat priority.

## Slow motion
If used, time dilation must be constrained and stylistically integrated. It cannot become a universal superpower that trivializes every fight.

## Weapon acquisition
The player begins adulthood with limited means. Important weapons are bought, traded, inherited, found in authored contexts or taken with consequence. No fountain of disposable guns.

## Sound
Every major firearm needs distinct near, mid and far reports, indoor/outdoor reflections and mechanical sounds. Gunfire should be heard at believable distances and influence AI where technically feasible.
