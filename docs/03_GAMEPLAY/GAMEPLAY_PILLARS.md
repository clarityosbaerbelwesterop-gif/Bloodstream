# BLOODSTREAM — GAMEPLAY PILLARS

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Core loop
Town / lead → preparation → travel → investigation/tracking → confrontation → outcome → consequence → recovery.

The loop must flex. Some bounties start in wilderness; some end without gunfire; some begin as unrelated discoveries.

## 1. Observation
The player reads terrain, people, weather, rooms and routines. Observation is an active skill supported by animation, sound, clue design and readable simulation—not a magic x-ray mode.

## 2. Pursuit
Targets move, hide, lie and react. Travel distance, weather, spoor and witness testimony matter. The player feels like a hunter rather than an icon follower.

## 3. Human tension
Conversation, waiting, bluffing, searching and uncertainty can carry entire sequences. The game celebrates the moment before violence.

## 4. Lethal gunplay
Weapons are dangerous. Positioning, first shot, cover, reload state, ammunition and injuries matter. Enemies value survival.

## 5. World survival
Food, rest, weather, clothing, shelter and wounds provide texture and decisions but must never become meters that need servicing every five minutes.

## 6. Camp and preparation
The camp is a portable planning space: cook, rest, maintain equipment, review evidence, craft limited field items, change clothing and reflect.

## 7. Relationships and consequence
People remember major actions. Reputation is local and contextual. Named characters have continuity.

## 8. Discovery
The player can find stories without quest markers through smoke, animal behavior, sound, tracks, architecture, maps, rumors and curiosity.

## Input feel
Movement should have physical weight without sluggishness. Interaction prioritizes context and consistency. Horse control must be polished enough to carry hours of travel.

## Difficulty
Difficulty settings should tune:
- aim assistance;
- enemy perception and accuracy;
- damage tolerance;
- clue assistance;
- survival severity;
- economy pressure;
- navigation guidance.

Do not make higher difficulty simply inflate enemy health.

## System interaction requirement
Every major system should interact with at least two others. Examples:
- weather affects tracking and travel;
- law pressure affects bounty routes and town behavior;
- injuries affect gun handling and camp recovery;
- animal carcasses affect scavengers and tracking;
- reputation affects witness cooperation and prices.

## Anti-grind rule
Progression comes from knowledge, equipment, relationships, experience and authored opportunities—not repetitive XP farming.
