# BLOODSTREAM — LAW, CRIME & REPUTATION

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Principle
Law is local, witnessed and political. The world should not instantly know what the player did in an empty canyon.

## Detection
Legal response depends on witnesses, identification, jurisdiction, evidence and communication. A masked or distant suspect may be described imperfectly. Telegraph and rail can spread information quickly between connected towns once the event is reported.

## Jurisdictions
Sheriffs, deputies, marshals, company guards and posses have different authority. Crossing a boundary may change who can pursue, but serious crimes can propagate through warrants.

## Wanted state
Wanted pressure is contextual rather than a single global star meter. A settlement or county can know the protagonist while another does not.

## Witness behavior
Witnesses may flee, hide, seek law, intervene, lie or refuse involvement based on personality and relationship. The player can sometimes talk down or correct misunderstandings before escalation.

## Reputation
Track authored/local impressions such as reliable hunter, dangerous gunman, oath-breaker, protector, company ally or troublemaker. Do not expose one universal morality number.

## Crime consequences
Consequences include fines, warrants, arrest attempts, denied services, higher prices, bounty hunters, testimony, rumor and changed relationships.

## Surrender/arrest
Where technically supported, the player may surrender, pay fines, spend/skip short custody, contest evidence through story contexts or escape. The system should avoid long repetitive punishment loops.

## Story immunity
Main-story missions should not silently erase player law state. When story staging needs temporary legal handling, document it explicitly and restore/transform state coherently.
