# BLOODSTREAM — CREATIVE DIRECTION

Status: AUTHORITATIVE CREATIVE BRIEF

## North star

Bloodstream should feel like a playable frontier epic where breathtaking scale, intimate human behavior and explosive violence coexist. The player should remember rooms, faces, weather and decisions—not just mission objectives.

## Reference grammar, not imitation

We draw lessons from Western cinema and great open-world games at the level of craft:
- long suspense before short violence;
- morally unstable alliances;
- expressive faces and hands;
- wide landscape compositions;
- silence and environmental sound;
- dry, character-based humor;
- iconic standoffs;
- travel as emotional pacing;
- locations that tell history through use and decay.

We do not recreate specific scenes, characters, dialogue, costumes, music themes or plot structures.

## Tone

The tone is adult, grounded and emotionally broad.

Bloodstream can be:
- terrifying in a confined snowbound building;
- joyful around a crowded meal;
- funny in a badly managed saloon dispute;
- lonely on a three-day ride;
- awe-inspiring at sunrise above a valley;
- ugly and immediate in a gunfight;
- tender in a small family memory.

The game must earn darkness through attachment. Tragedy without prior warmth is noise.

## The first-hours promise

The opening must make the player's childhood home feel like home before it is attacked. The player spends meaningful time with parents, siblings, elders, children and ordinary work. The opening teaches interaction, observation, movement, hunting fundamentals, family dynamics and cultural life without feeling like a tutorial checklist.

The catastrophe then changes the meaning of places, objects, music and habits the player already knows.

## Adult protagonist

The adult protagonist is capable but not emotionally dead. He can:
- make dry jokes;
- enjoy food and music;
- care for animals;
- form attachments reluctantly;
- be patient with children;
- admire beauty;
- make serious moral mistakes;
- confuse revenge with justice.

His competence is earned over time and shown through animation, observation and choice rather than superhero behavior.

## Violence

Violence is not the background texture of every minute. When it comes, it should be readable, dangerous and consequential.

Gunfights prioritize:
- low time-to-kill;
- strong weapon identity;
- suppression and fear;
- meaningful cover and movement;
- limited ammunition during extended encounters;
- injuries that affect behavior;
- surrender, retreat and capture as valid outcomes.

## Beauty

The world must routinely create moments that require no objective.

Examples:
- fog lifting from a river at dawn;
- distant bison crossing a plain;
- snow crystals moving across a pass;
- a thunderstorm rolling toward a mining town;
- insects and fireflies above wetlands;
- aurora-like high-latitude sky effects only if geographically justified;
- wind moving an entire grassland in visible waves;
- distant train smoke revealing human presence.

Music should know when not to play.

## Pacing rule

Never stack major emotional peaks without decompression. After a brutal sequence, prefer travel, conversation, recovery, investigation or environmental discovery before the next major combat set piece.

## Iconic Bloodstream image

A lone rider on a ridge above a vast frontier, carrying a folded bounty notice and the memory of a family that once made the world feel safe.

That image should be emotionally true even after fifty hours.
