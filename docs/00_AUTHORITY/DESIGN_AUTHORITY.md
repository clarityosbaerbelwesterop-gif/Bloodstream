# BLOODSTREAM — DESIGN AUTHORITY

Status: AUTHORITATIVE FOUNDATION

## 1. Product identity

Bloodstream is a large-scale, single-player, third-person open-world Western action-adventure built around bounty hunting, tracking, human tension, survival, travel and authored story.

The project may evoke the emotional grammar of classic and revisionist Western cinema—especially long-form tension, morally unstable confrontations, silence, landscape, black humor and sudden violence—but it must not copy protected characters, scenes, dialogue, story beats, iconography, music or compositions from any existing film or game.

The game must feel like Bloodstream first.

## 2. Core fantasy

The player inhabits an Indigenous survivor who grows from a loved child inside a living family and community into an exceptionally capable frontier hunter. He pursues people connected to the destruction of his home, but gradually discovers that the violence was part of a larger economic and political machine.

The player fantasy is not "be the deadliest outlaw." It is:

> Read the land. Read people. Find the truth. Decide what justice costs.

## 3. Emotional thesis

Bloodstream is not relentlessly miserable. Its emotional power depends on contrast.

The world must be:
- cruel enough that despair is understandable;
- beautiful enough that life remains worth defending;
- warm enough that loss matters;
- funny enough that people feel human;
- quiet enough that violence retains weight.

Five required emotional states shape the game:
1. **Wonder** — landscape, wildlife, weather, discovery.
2. **Warmth** — family, community, friendship, food, music, humor.
3. **Melancholy** — memory, solitude, distance, aging, absence.
4. **Tension** — suspicion, negotiation, duels, confined spaces, pursuit.
5. **Brutality** — short, consequential violence rather than constant spectacle.

No single state may dominate the complete game.

## 4. Non-negotiable design pillars

1. **Authored Western situations over filler.** No map made of repeated camps and identical objectives.
2. **Bounty hunting is investigation.** Targets are learned, tracked, understood and located rather than revealed by omniscient UI.
3. **Landscape is gameplay.** Terrain, weather, tracks, sight lines, wildlife and distance matter.
4. **Violence is fast and dangerous.** Firearms are lethal, ammunition matters, wounds and positioning matter.
5. **Tension before gunfire.** Conversation, waiting, uncertainty and observation are valid gameplay.
6. **The camp is a living reset point.** Rest, cooking, maintenance, planning and reflection support travel without becoming tedious chores.
7. **People have continuity.** Major NPCs have schedules, relationships, memory and believable motives.
8. **The protagonist is morally complex but not nihilistic.** Trauma does not erase humor, love, kindness or curiosity.
9. **Culture is living, not decorative.** Indigenous identity cannot exist only to justify a revenge plot.
10. **No omnipresent HUD.** Information should come from the world whenever practical.

## 5. Scope philosophy

Bloodstream aims to feel enormous through density, distance, authored variation and systemic interactions—not by raw square kilometers or meaningless content counts.

Prefer:
- 30–50 memorable major bounties over hundreds of generic targets;
- distinctive settlements over dozens of interchangeable towns;
- fewer, deeper systems that interact;
- handcrafted landmark spaces inside systemic landscapes;
- meaningful travel with changing conditions.

## 6. Story authority

Working canon:
- Main antagonist: **Frank "The Judge" Vance**.
- Vance led or commanded the force responsible for the protagonist's childhood catastrophe.
- Vance is not the ultimate source of the violence; he is a willing instrument of larger commercial and political interests.
- The protagonist's exact nation, language, personal name and cultural details remain research-gated until a dedicated authenticity pass confirms a respectful, historically coherent choice.
- The childhood community must be shown as alive, funny, flawed, loving and culturally specific before the attack.
- Survivors must exist. The community cannot be reduced to dead motivation.
- Sexual violence, if retained in canon, is never interactive, voyeuristic or graphically staged; its narrative weight comes through consequence, survivor perspective and restraint.

## 7. Open-world authority

The world is contiguous unless technical evidence forces a different solution. It must support:
- temperate frontier lowlands;
- mining country;
- dense forest;
- high mountains and snow;
- dry southern frontier/desert;
- river/wetland territory;
- rail and road corridors;
- isolated homesteads, forts, mines, stations and waypoints.

Major spaces are authored. Wilderness connective tissue may use procedural tools during production, but final shipping geography must be art-directed and gameplay-validated.

## 8. No-slop rules

Forbidden as default content:
- identical bandit camps with changed names;
- floating icons leading directly to every secret;
- random loot showers;
- enemies spawned solely to fill traversal time;
- generic "clear area" objectives repeated without narrative reason;
- exposition NPCs who exist only to dump lore;
- morality meters that reduce complex decisions to blue/red points;
- invincible story characters outside justified staging;
- comedy that destroys dramatic credibility;
- misery used as a substitute for characterization.

## 9. Player respect

The game trusts the player to observe, infer and remember. It can provide accessibility options without flattening the default experience. Navigation, tracking and clue density should be adjustable.

## 10. Change control

If another document conflicts with this file, this file wins unless this file is deliberately amended.

Any future change to protagonist identity, era, core antagonist, world structure, emotional thesis or the ten design pillars requires an explicit canon change recorded in the repository.
