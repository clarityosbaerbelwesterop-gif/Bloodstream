# BLOODSTREAM — PROLOGUE: FAMILY & COMMUNITY

Status: AUTHORITATIVE OPENING DESIGN

## Goal
Make the childhood home feel genuinely loved before anything is lost. The player should know the family as people, not exposition.

Target first-play duration: approximately 60–90 minutes, adjustable after testing.

## Opening rhythm

### Morning — safety
The child wakes to ordinary life. Movement and interaction are learned through chores, teasing, small choices and exploration.

### Midday — belonging
The player spends meaningful time with parents, siblings, peers and elders. Hunting/tracking fundamentals arrive through a low-stakes outing. The community is social, imperfect and alive.

### Evening — memory anchor
A meal, song, story, game or gathering creates recurring emotional motifs. The player helps make or receives one small personal object that survives into adulthood.

### Warning
Before the attack, subtle signs appear: an overdue traveler, distant smoke, missing livestock, unfamiliar tracks or a tense adult conversation. The player should feel that something has shifted without knowing exactly what.

### Catastrophe
The attack arrives with confusion rather than heroic staging. The player is a child and cannot solve it with combat. Objectives center on finding family, hiding, helping where plausible, escaping and surviving.

## Family design
Exact names remain RESEARCH-GATED until protagonist culture is locked.

Required roles:
- **Parent A:** teaches a practical outdoor skill and has a private, unexpectedly gentle side.
- **Parent B:** emotional/social center of the household with humor and a recurring food/music motif.
- **Older sibling:** confidant and rival who knows how to provoke and encourage the protagonist.
- **Younger sibling:** affectionate nuisance who creates play and responsibility.
- **Extended relative/guardian:** broadens family structure where culturally appropriate.

At least two family members must have personal goals unrelated to the protagonist.

## Community life
The player should see and participate in food preparation, craft/repair, children at play, animal care, trade, music, storytelling, arguments, work and reconciliation. At least ten faces should be recognizable before the catastrophe.

## Emotional anchors
Use 3–5 motifs across the game: a melody, private joke, food preparation, handcrafted object, viewpoint or hunting lesson. Do not repeat them constantly; absence creates power.

## Attack rules
The attackers are frightening because the player understands what is being damaged.
- no child power fantasy;
- no prolonged graphic suffering;
- use perspective, sound, smoke, separation and uncertainty;
- sexual violence, if retained in canon, is never interactive or graphically shown;
- aftermath and survivor perspective receive real narrative time.

## Transition to adulthood
Do not jump straight from trauma to an invincible adult. Show displacement and survival, then use carefully selected time transitions to establish work, training, relationships and accumulated skills.
