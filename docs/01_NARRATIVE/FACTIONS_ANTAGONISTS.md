# BLOODSTREAM — FACTIONS & ANTAGONISTS

Status: WORKING CANON

## Design rule
Factions are economic and social organisms, not color-coded enemy teams. Each needs income, territory, internal disagreement, relationships and a reason to exist.

## Vance Organization
A flexible network of gunmen, scouts, contract enforcers and fixers, not a uniform army occupying repeated camps.

Typical work:
- rail protection and land clearing;
- mine security and strikebreaking;
- debt collection;
- prisoner/valuable transport;
- political intimidation;
- private bounty contracts.

Its cells are compartmentalized so one captured member reveals pieces, not the entire network.

## Mining combines
Competing companies around Lindstripe and mountain territory. They control claims, housing, stores and debt. Some hire Vance; others oppose him only when his actions threaten business.

Gameplay spaces include labor disputes, missing workers, sabotage, cave-ins, payroll transport, company towns and bribed law.

## Rail company
The railroad transforms the physical and political world. It brings mobility and trade while enabling land seizure, private security and rapid concentration of force.

Gameplay: trains, schedules, telegraph, contracts, prisoner cars, mobile targets, bridges, tunnels and station politics.

## Local law
No universal police faction. Authority changes by jurisdiction: sheriffs, deputies, marshals, posses, company guards and other historically justified bodies. Law can be honest, corrupt, frightened or politically trapped.

## Settler/farming communities
Not a monolith. Poor farmers, ranchers, migrants, laborers, families and opportunists have distinct interests. Some benefit from dispossession; others are exploited by the same companies.

## Indigenous communities
If multiple communities are used, each must be historically/geographically appropriate after research. They are not one alliance or worldview. Survival strategies, politics, trade and opinions of the protagonist vary.

## Bounty hunters
An informal professional ecosystem rather than a faction army. Hunters may compete, sell information, betray agreements, rescue the player for leverage, form temporary alliances or die in authored circumstances.

## Outlaw groups
Every major outlaw group needs a distinct economy and identity—cattle theft, train robbery, smuggling, extortion, deserters, protection rackets, etc. No generic "bandit faction."

## Antagonist ladder
The campaign escalates responsibility from killer → organizer → protector → client → institution. Bloodstream must make the player feel the difference between shooting a man and changing a system.
