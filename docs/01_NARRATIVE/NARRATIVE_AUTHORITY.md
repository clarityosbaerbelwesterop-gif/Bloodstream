# BLOODSTREAM — NARRATIVE AUTHORITY

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Premise
A child survives the destruction of his home and grows into a frontier hunter. Years later, a bounty board in the mining town of Lindstripe exposes faces connected to the old crime. What begins as a pursuit of monsters becomes an investigation into who profited from them.

## Narrative promise
The story is about revenge, justice, memory and the systems that turn private greed into public violence.

It must answer:
1. What does the protagonist want when revenge stops being abstract?
2. What happens when some guilty people are human enough to complicate hatred?
3. Can a person built around pursuit choose a life that is not pursuit?

## Long-form structure
- Prologue: Home.
- Act I: Survival and professional life.
- Act II: Lindstripe and the first recognizable faces.
- Act III: The Vance network.
- Act IV: Fracture—truth, betrayal and competing hunters.
- Act V: The larger machine—rail, mining, law and political protection.
- Act VI: Vance.
- Act VII: After Vance—proof that killing one man does not erase the system.
- Finale: consequence and a future not reducible to revenge.

## Frank "The Judge" Vance
Vance is charismatic, disciplined and morally empty in a practical rather than theatrical way. He views people, land and violence as costs in transactions. He led or commanded the force responsible for the opening catastrophe while working for commercial interests seeking land, routes or mineral access.

Vance is not omnipotent. His power comes from money, fear, contracts, corrupt officials, hired gunmen and reputation.

## The wider antagonist
The responsibility chain runs from field killers to organizers, protectors, clients and institutions. The player can kill individuals; the story asks whether institutions can be confronted the same way.

## Moral design
No global good/evil meter. Choices are remembered through world state, relationships and consequences. The protagonist has a defined personality, but the player controls methods and some outcomes.

Potential decisions include kill/capture/release, expose/blackmail/destroy evidence, protect a witness or preserve access to a target, accept help from compromised allies, and choose immediate revenge versus preventing harm elsewhere.

## Survivors
The opening community has survivors. At least three become meaningful later characters with different relationships to the protagonist's path: one prioritizes collective survival, one shares the desire for vengeance but questions methods, and one rejects living in the past.

## Ending principle
Killing Vance cannot magically heal trauma. Emotional resolution comes from what the protagonist chooses to protect, build or leave behind. No ending treats disappearance or isolation as the only noble future.
