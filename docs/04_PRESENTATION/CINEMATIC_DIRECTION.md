# BLOODSTREAM — CINEMATIC DIRECTION

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## Philosophy
Cinematics protect performance and composition without turning the game into a movie that constantly interrupts play.

## Camera grammar
Use wide landscape frames, patient two-shots, close attention to eyes and hands, doorways and windows as frames, selective low angles and restrained camera movement. Avoid nonstop handheld shake and hyperactive cutting.

## Tension
Long takes and silence can be more valuable than dialogue. In confrontations, geography remains clear so the player understands doors, windows, witnesses and distances before control returns.

## Violence
Impact comes from anticipation, speed, reaction and aftermath rather than lingering spectacle. Graphic intensity must be adjustable.

## Family opening
The camera stays close enough to make home tactile but must not signal doom through every composition. Warmth is allowed to exist sincerely.

## Gameplay transitions
Prefer seamless transitions where the player's current position and equipment are respected. Avoid teleporting the protagonist to a cinematic mark without justification.

## Performance
Facial and body acting carry subtext. Characters can pause, fail to answer, look at someone else or continue a physical task while speaking.

## Dialogue coverage
Do not default to shot/reverse-shot for every exchange. Use blocking, shared space and changing focus.

## Signature confrontations
Duels and multi-person standoffs receive bespoke camera logic during the pre-draw phase, but player control and readability remain primary.

## Cutscene budget
Reserve long cinematics for emotional or structural events. Routine briefings, travel and information handoff should often happen interactively.
