# BLOODSTREAM — UI, UX & ACCESSIBILITY

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## UI thesis
The world should carry information whenever possible. UI exists to clarify, not to replace observation.

## Default HUD
Keep the default HUD restrained. Core states may appear contextually rather than permanently. Avoid a crowded minimap full of icons.

## Navigation
Primary tools are a physical-style map, road signs, landmarks, journal notes, railway information, compass support where historically/design appropriate and optional directional assistance.

Accessibility may add stronger route guidance, objective markers or clue highlighting without changing canon.

## Bounty dossier
The bounty interface behaves like a working notebook: portrait/sketch where available, aliases, crimes, last known information, source reliability, clues, witnesses and player annotations/automatic authored notes.

## Journal
The journal stores maps, sketches, major discoveries, people and unresolved leads. It should feel authored and useful rather than a completion checklist.

## Inventory
Prioritize readable categories and physical logic. Immediate gear differs from horse/camp storage. Do not require excessive menu navigation to perform common actions.

## Interaction prompts
Prompts are small, consistent and context-sensitive. Repeated familiar actions may reduce prompt prominence.

## Accessibility baseline
Plan from the start for:
- subtitle size/background/speaker labels;
- remappable controls where platform permits;
- hold/toggle alternatives;
- aim assistance levels;
- clue/tracking assistance;
- navigation assistance;
- contrast/readability settings;
- color-independent information;
- reduced camera shake;
- reduced motion blur;
- reduced visual intensity for injuries and graphic effects;
- audio balancing and directional cues;
- adjustable survival friction;
- generous timing alternatives for duels/QTE-like moments.

## Cinematic subtitles
Subtitles must preserve timing and attribution without covering critical interaction space.

## No fake immersion
Never sacrifice basic usability merely to claim the UI is diegetic. Immersion and accessibility are compatible when the system offers layered presentation.
