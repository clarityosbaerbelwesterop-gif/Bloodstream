# BLOODSTREAM — TOWNS & SETTLEMENTS

Status: WORKING CANON

## Settlement rule
Every town has an economy, power structure, visual identity, daily rhythm and surrounding resource network. A town is not a shop cluster.

## Lindstripe — primary early hub
A rough mining and rail town where agriculture, extraction and opportunism collide.

Required districts:
- muddy main street with saloons, hotel, gunsmith/pawnbroker and sheriff;
- mine-company offices and worker housing;
- rail depot/freight yard;
- market/stock pens;
- poorer edge settlement;
- nearby shafts/quarries and creek crossings.

Narrative function: the protagonist's first stable adult hub and the place where old faces appear on a bounty board.

## Iron Junction — rail company town
A planned settlement built around locomotive servicing, freight and company control. Cleaner than Lindstripe but more surveilled.

Function: trains, security contracts, prisoner transport and labor politics.

## Mercy Crossing — river town
Busy ferry/bridge settlement connecting trade routes. Inns, warehouses, boat traffic and information brokerage.

Function: rumors, smuggling, changing jurisdiction and wetland access.

## Saint's Rest — mountain waypoint
Small settlement at the edge of White Crown territory. Outfitters, stable, doctor, chapel/meeting hall and weather station/telegraph where appropriate.

Function: preparation for high-country travel and snowbound story sequences.

## Carrion Gulch — Red Mesa settlement
Former boomtown that partially collapsed after a mine failed. Surviving businesses serve travelers, prospectors and criminals.

Function: decayed prosperity, hidden tunnels, duels and desert bounty routes.

## Southwatch — southern trade town
Hot, crowded, multilingual commerce hub with competing legal authorities and constant movement.

Function: border trade, smugglers, hired guns and three-way faction pressure.

## Smaller settlement types
- ranch clusters;
- Indigenous communities researched and authored separately;
- logging camps;
- mine camps;
- rail work camps;
- military/abandoned forts when historically justified;
- stage/telegraph stations;
- river landings;
- isolated lodges;
- family homesteads.

## Daily life requirements
Major settlements simulate morning opening, work shifts, meal periods, evening entertainment and night reduction. NPC density changes with weather, paydays, train arrivals and story events.

## Interiors
Important buildings must have functional layouts. Avoid fake doors everywhere in central spaces. Not every house needs an interior, but the player should learn which structures are real through visual language rather than arbitrary interaction prompts.

## Growth and damage
Selected settlements visibly change over chapters: construction, closures, strikes, funerals, rebuilding, new rail, ownership changes and population shifts.
