# BLOODSTREAM — WORLD BIBLE

Status: DOMAIN AUTHORITY

## World concept
Bloodstream takes place in a fictionalized western frontier territory assembled from historically compatible environments rather than a literal copy of one state. Geography must feel continuous, economically legible and worth crossing on horseback.

The world is not a theme park of biomes. Rivers, mountain ranges, rain shadows, rail lines, trade routes and settlement economies must explain transitions.

## Scale philosophy
The world should feel immense because travel has rhythm and distance, not because empty kilometers separate icons.

Target principles:
- one continuous primary world unless technical evidence proves otherwise;
- 8–12 major settlements with distinct economies and social identities;
- smaller stations, villages, forts, homesteads, camps, mines and waypoints;
- large wilderness zones where encountering nobody is normal;
- landmark visibility that helps orientation;
- enough travel time for weather, conversation, tracking and camp decisions to matter.

## Geographic spine
Working west-to-east/north-to-south structure:
- **White Crown Range:** high mountains, deep snow, passes and isolated lodges;
- **Iron Vale:** mines, rail construction, logging and company pressure;
- **Lindstripe Basin:** central mining/agricultural crossroads and early adult-game hub;
- **Alder Country:** temperate forest, rivers, ranches and hunting terrain;
- **Blackwater Reach:** wetlands, river trade, mosquitoes, fog and hidden channels;
- **Red Mesa Frontier:** dry plateaus, canyons, forts and outlaw routes;
- **Southern Dustlands:** hottest frontier zone, long sight lines, border commerce and smuggling.

Names are WORKING CANON pending map pass.

## Human infrastructure
Every major settlement exists for a reason: mine, rail junction, river crossing, ranching, fort, timber, trade, administration or pilgrimage/cultural continuity where appropriate.

Road quality, bridge placement and train access reflect wealth and political power.

## Wilderness density
Use three density modes:
- **settled:** frequent farms, roads, traffic, patrols and commerce;
- **frontier:** long distances between human sites, occasional travelers;
- **deep wild:** few roads, strong wildlife presence, difficult weather and navigation.

## Landmark design
Each region needs 5–10 major silhouette landmarks visible from distance and 20–40 local points of interest with authored purpose.

Landmarks should include natural formations, bridges, mine works, ruined structures, towers, distinctive trees, ridges, waterfalls, rail structures and culturally grounded sites.

## Discovery
No mandatory tower-reveal system. Players discover through roads, maps, conversation, smoke, sound, tracks, railway timetables, signage, high ground and curiosity.

## World evolution
The map changes over the campaign:
- rail construction advances;
- mines expand, close or collapse;
- weather damages roads/bridges;
- businesses change ownership;
- wanted posters and political notices update;
- certain burned or damaged places rebuild;
- patrol and outlaw pressure responds to story state.

## Travel expectation
A major cross-map journey should feel like a journey, but not a punishment. Fast travel exists only after the player establishes believable transport connections and may be presented through trains, stagecoaches or camp travel rather than teleport UI.
