# BLOODSTREAM — WORLD EVENTS & ENCOUNTERS

Status: SYSTEMIC CONTENT STANDARD

## Goal
Open-world encounters should create stories rather than announce themselves as repeating activities.

## Encounter families
- travelers needing help or information;
- stranded wagons;
- injured riders;
- hunters and trappers;
- law transporting prisoners;
- funeral/procession;
- arguments escalating in public;
- animal incidents;
- weather refuge situations;
- train/road breakdowns;
- scouts watching from distance;
- rival bounty hunters following the same clue;
- company guards confronting workers;
- missing-person traces;
- abandoned camps with environmental stories;
- ambushes used sparingly and logically.

## Authored + systemic blend
Use authored encounter templates with contextual variation, persistent participants where possible and regional conditions. Pure random spawning should never produce nonsense such as the same victim being attacked every ride.

## Memory
Selected encounter NPCs can reappear later. Helping someone may produce a rumor, discount, letter, testimony, rescue or new information rather than an immediate loot box.

## Escalation
Encounters should not all become gunfights. Possible resolutions include observation, conversation, trade, intimidation, first aid, repair, escort, avoidance, reporting to law or leaving the situation alone.

## Rarity and spacing
Quiet travel is valuable. The encounter director must enforce cooldowns and regional density. Deep wilderness may produce long stretches with only weather and animals.

## Discovery without icons
Smoke, gunshots, bells, circling scavengers, stopped traffic, distant shouting, unusual tracks or a rider leaving the road can draw attention.

## Consequence
Events can alter later state: a saved traveler returns home, a prisoner escapes, a broken wagon remains for a time, a body draws law/scavengers, a burned building is later repaired.

## Content quality gate
Before adding an encounter, ask:
1. Does it reveal something about this region or people?
2. Does it have more than one plausible player response?
3. Can it be recognized as a copy of an earlier event?
4. Will it still feel believable on a second playthrough?

If the answer to #1 or #2 is no, redesign it.
